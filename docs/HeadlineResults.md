## Single terms and augmentation

- **Single-term units.** For any semiring `R` and monoid `M` in independent
  universes, a unit `single m r` has a unit coefficient `r`, even when `R` is
  trivial. Over a **nontrivial** semiring, the
  [basis embedding reflects units](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/Units.lean), and
  `IsUnit (single m r) ↔ IsUnit r ∧ IsUnit m`. No ring, commutativity, group,
  cancellation, domain or finiteness hypothesis is required. This criterion
  concerns an already-single term; the [arbitrary-element classification](#units-with-two-unique-products)
  has additional hypotheses.

- **Augmentation kernel generators.** For any possibly noncommutative ring `R`
  and any monoid `M` in independent universes, the
  [augmentation kernel](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/Augmentation.lean) in
  `MonoidAlgebra R M` is the **left** ideal generated by `single m 1 - 1`.
  The coefficient-sum augmentation is a ring homomorphism already over any
  semiring. Its coefficient-sum and single-term evaluation laws give its public
  API. Neither commutativity, inverses, finiteness nor nontriviality is assumed.
  A [monoid generating set](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationGenerators.lean)
  `S` already suffices: the kernel is the left span of `single s 1 - 1` for
  `s ∈ S` when `Submonoid.closure S = ⊤`. For a group, the weaker hypothesis
  `Subgroup.closure S = ⊤` suffices for the same span; no normal closure is used.

- **Augmentation-kernel coefficient basis.** For any possibly noncommutative
  ring `R` and monoid `M` in independent universes,
  [`MonoidAlgebra.augmentationKerBasis`](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationBasis.lean)
  is a basis of `(RingHom.ker (MonoidAlgebra.augmentation R M)).restrictScalars R`
  over the **left coefficient ring `R`**, not over `R[M]`. Its index type is
  `{m : M // m ≠ 1}`, its vector is `single m 1 - 1`, and the coordinate of a
  kernel element at `m` is its coefficient at `m`. No group, commutativity,
  cancellation, finiteness, nontriviality or decidable-equality premise is added.
  The zero ring and singleton monoids are allowed. The generic engine
  [`Finsupp.kerLsumEquiv`](../GroupRings/LinearAlgebra/Finsupp/SumKernel.lean)
  works for any semiring acting on an additive commutative group: restrict away
  from a chosen point, and invert by extension minus the coefficient sum at that
  point. A semiring coefficient basis assertion would be false in general.

- **Necessary conditions for augmentation projectivity.** For any nontrivial
  semiring `R` and any group `G` in independent universes,
  [`MonoidAlgebra.finite_and_isUnit_natCard_of_projective_augmentation`](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationProjective.lean)
  states that projectivity of `R` as a **left `R[G]`-semimodule** with action
  `a • r = MonoidAlgebra.augmentation R G a * r` implies
  `Finite G ∧ IsUnit (Nat.card G : R)`. This is the coefficient carrier with
  the canonical augmentation action, not the regular `R[G]`-module or an
  arbitrary action on `R`. Neither coefficient commutativity, additive inverses,
  a domain condition nor group finiteness is assumed. Nontriviality matters:
  over the zero semiring the coefficient semimodule is projective even for an
  infinite group. This theorem asserts necessity, not a converse construction.

## Units with two unique products

For `[Semiring R] [NoZeroDivisors R] [Monoid M] [TwoUniqueProds M]` in
independent universes,
[`MonoidAlgebra.isUnit_iff_exists_single_units`](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/UnitsUniqueProds.lean)
classifies arbitrary elements `f : MonoidAlgebra R M`:

```lean
IsUnit f ↔ ∃ (m : Units M) (r : Units R),
  f = MonoidAlgebra.single m.val r.val
```

In this multiplicative-index statement, index, coefficient and algebra units
are all multiplicative. Neither coefficient commutativity, a group or order on
`M`, a ring structure, finiteness nor nontriviality of `R` is assumed. The
product is canonical convolution, not pointwise multiplication.

For `[AddMonoid A] [TwoUniqueSums A]` with the same coefficient hypotheses,
[`AddMonoidAlgebra.isUnit_iff_exists_single_units`](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/UnitsUniqueProds.lean)
states, for `f : AddMonoidAlgebra R A`, the additive-index counterpart:

```lean
IsUnit f ↔ ∃ (a : AddUnits A) (r : Units R),
  f = AddMonoidAlgebra.single a.val r.val
```

Only the index units are additive. Coefficient and algebra units are still
multiplicative; the algebra identity is `1`, at index `0` with coefficient `1`.
This counterpart reuses the multiplicative classification through Mathlib's
coefficient-preserving index-tag ring equivalence.

The zero semiring is included: every algebra element equals
`single (1 : M) (1 : R)`, so the identity units supply the existential witnesses.
Its coefficient support is empty, not of cardinality one. The theorem does
not reflect unit status of an arbitrary index appearing in a single-term
presentation over zero coefficients. With nontrivial coefficients a unit does
have support cardinality one. In contrast, the already-single criterion
`MonoidAlgebra.isUnit_single_iff` requires `Nontrivial R` but works for **any**
monoid, with neither `NoZeroDivisors R` nor `TwoUniqueProds M`.
See the [usage guide](Usage.md#units-with-two-unique-products) and
[formalization credits](../CREDITS.md#units-and-unique-products).

## Convolution support

For any `[Semiring R] [NoZeroDivisors R]` and independently polymorphic
`[Mul M] [TwoUniqueProds M]`,
[`MonoidAlgebra.one_lt_card_support_coeff_mul`](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/SupportUniqueProds.lean)
states that, for `f g : R[M]`,
`1 < f.coeff.support.card * g.coeff.support.card` implies
`1 < (f * g).coeff.support.card`. The product is canonical convolution;
the premise multiplies the two input support cardinalities, not the number
of distinct product labels. Neither coefficient commutativity nor an index
identity or associativity is assumed. Zero coefficient semirings are allowed:
the premise then cannot hold. This is a support estimate; with a monoid
identity and associativity it supplies the support step in the
[unit classification](#units-with-two-unique-products).

## Coefficient tests and finite matrices

- **Product-coefficient separation.** For any group over a possibly zero or
  noncommutative semiring, fix one arbitrary group index. Two group-algebra
  elements agree exactly when their coefficients at that index agree after every
  right multiplication; independently, every left multiplication gives the same
  criterion. Either family detects zero. The identity coefficient is the index
  `1` case, and no finite-group assumption or augmentation map is required. See
  [ProductCoefficients.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/ProductCoefficients.lean).

- **Centers of group algebras.** Over any possibly zero or noncommutative semiring,
  a group-algebra element is central exactly when every coefficient is central and
  its coefficients are invariant under conjugation. A nonzero coefficient of a
  central element forces that label's conjugation orbit to be finite; coefficients
  therefore vanish on infinite conjugation orbits. Coefficient centrality also
  holds for monoid algebras, and the identity-group and commutative-coefficient
  reductions are included in
  [`MonoidAlgebra/Center.lean`](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/Center.lean).

- **Finite matrices and monoid algebras.** For any semiring `R`, monoid `M`
  and finite decidable index type `n`, there is a canonical ring equivalence
  `MonoidAlgebra (Matrix n n R) M ≃+* Matrix n n (MonoidAlgebra R M)`.
  It preserves every matrix entry of every monoid coefficient, with forward
  and inverse coefficient and single-term simplification laws. Ordinary matrix
  multiplication and monoid convolution retain both factor orders. The three
  universes are independent; empty indices and zero or noncommutative
  coefficients are allowed. See [Matrix.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/Matrix.lean).

## Crossed maps and averaging

- **Crossed maps and the full augmentation ideal.** For any ring `R`, monoid `M`,
  and unital left `R[M]`-module `V`, the `R[M]`-linear maps from the full
  augmentation ideal `I` to `V` are additively equivalent to normalized maps
  `z : M → V` satisfying `z(mn) = z(m) + [m] • z(n)`, with the canonical
  action `[m] • v`. The forward map is `z(m) = F([m] - 1)`. See
  [AugmentationCrossedUniversal.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationCrossedUniversal.lean).

- **Principal crossed maps for given group-algebra modules.** If a finite group
  has unit order in a possibly noncommutative or zero ring, every crossed map
  for a given unital left group-algebra module is principal. The witness is the
  negative inverse-order sum, and this sum construction commutes with additive
  maps. Every group-algebra-linear map from the full augmentation ideal is
  induced by the action on a vector. See
  [AugmentationAveraging.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationAveraging.lean).

## Augmentation cotangents

- **Augmentation cotangents.** For any unital ring `R`, monoid `M` and left
  `R`-module `V`, linear maps from `I/I²` to `V` are naturally equivalent, as a
  **set**, to monoid homomorphisms `M →* Multiplicative V`. For a group `G`,
  there is an **`R`-linear equivalence**
  `I/I² ≃ₗ[R] R ⊗[ℤ] Additive (Abelianization G)`.
  Here `I` is the augmentation ideal and `I²` its ordinary ideal product square.
  No commutativity of `R` or `M`, or freeness of `G` or its abelianization, is
  assumed. See the [universal-property module](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationCotangent/Universal.lean),
  [group tensor module](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationCotangent/Tensor.lean)
  and [augmentation-cotangent guide](AugmentationCotangent.md).

- **Cotangent covariance and products.** For arbitrary unital rings, coefficient
  change is semilinear and commutes with monoid covariance. The group comparison
  with `R ⊗[ℤ] Additive (Abelianization G)` is natural for both variables.
  For any two monoids, `Q_R(M × N) ≃ₗ[R] Q_R(M) × Q_R(N)` via projections and
  the sum of inclusions. See the [cotangent guide](AugmentationCotangent.md)
  and `MonoidAlgebra.augmentationCotangentProdEquiv`.

## Finite p-groups and fixed vectors

- **Augmentation nilpotence and group finiteness.** For any possibly
  noncommutative ring `R` and any group `G`, [nilpotence of the augmentation
  kernel forces `Subsingleton R ∨ Finite G`](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationNilpotence/Converse.lean),
  with no characteristic assumption. For a prime `p` with `(p : R) = 0`,
  nilpotence is equivalent to `Subsingleton R ∨ (Finite G ∧ IsPGroup p G)`,
  with no finiteness hypothesis on `G`. Under these hypotheses, a finite
  `p`-group `G` has [augmentation kernel with zero
  `Nat.card G`-th power](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationNilpotence/Finite.lean).
  Thus every mixed ordered product of that length vanishes, including over
  the zero coefficient ring. For **nontrivial** `R`, nilpotence moreover
  implies `∃ k, ∀ g : G, g ^ (p ^ k) = 1`. The supporting left-ideal API
  over **any semiring** identifies zero `n`-th powers with vanishing of all
  length-`n` ordered products, including degree zero. See the
  [augmentation-nilpotence guide](AugmentationNilpotence.md).

- **Elementwise nilpotence for locally finite `p`-group algebras.** Let `R`
  be any possibly noncommutative or zero ring, `p` a prime with `(p : R) = 0`,
  and `G` a group with `IsPGroup p G` such that every finite subset generates
  a finite subgroup, expressed as
  `∀ s : Finset G, Finite (Subgroup.closure (s : Set G))`.
  Every augmentation-zero element of `R[G]` is nilpotent; the exponent may
  depend on the element, and no vanishing power of the whole kernel is asserted.
  The coefficient and group universes are independent; `G` need not be finite
  or abelian. See
  [LocallyFinite.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationNilpotence/LocallyFinite.lean).

- **Jacobson radicals of locally finite `p`-group algebras.** Under the same
  hypotheses on `R`, `p` and `G`,
  `Ring.jacobson R[G] = Ideal.comap (augmentation R G) (Ring.jacobson R)`.
  Here `Ring.jacobson` is the two-sided Jacobson radical, represented as an
  `Ideal`, even for noncommutative rings. Thus `x` lies in the radical exactly
  when its coefficient sum does, and `single g r` lies in the radical exactly
  when `r` does. The case `g = 1` is the coefficient-inclusion criterion.
  If `Ring.jacobson R = ⊥`, the radical of `R[G]` equals the augmentation
  kernel. These statements require neither a uniform nilpotence exponent nor
  a vanishing power of that kernel. See [Jacobson.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/Jacobson.lean)
  and the [radical guide](Jacobson.md).

- **Common annihilators and fixed vectors.** If one power of an ordinary left
  ideal over any semiring annihilates a nontrivial semimodule, there is a
  nonzero vector killed by the whole ideal. Two-sided ideals have a canonical
  common-annihilator submodule, agreeing with commutative set torsion. For a
  finite `p`-group over any possibly noncommutative ring with prime `p = 0`,
  every nontrivial unital group-algebra module has a nonzero vector fixed by
  all canonical group symbols. No field, coefficient commutativity, `CharP`,
  finite generation, dimension or finite-module hypothesis is needed. See the
  [common-annihilator and fixed-vector guide](FixedVectors.md).

- **Simple modules of finite `p`-group algebras.** For any possibly
  noncommutative or zero ring `R`, prime `p` with `(p : R) = 0`, finite
  `p`-group `G` and unital left `R[G]`-module `V`, simplicity over `R[G]`
  is equivalent to simplicity over `R` under canonical coefficient
  restriction. A simple module has augmentation action: every group basis
  element fixes every vector. No finiteness assumption on `V`, field or
  additional nontriviality hypothesis is needed. The characterization of
  augmentation action by trivial monoid action holds over any semiring.
  See the [simple-module guide](SimpleModules.md) and
  [module](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/SimpleModules.lean).

## Given modules and scalar restriction

- **Normalized norm on given group-algebra modules.** Let `G` be finite and
  let its order be a unit in any possibly noncommutative or zero semiring `R`.
  On every given `R[G]`-module whose addition is a commutative monoid, the
  normalized norm is an `R[G]`-linear idempotent. It fixes exactly the vectors
  fixed by all `single g 1`, and its range is their full `R[G]`-span. Every unit
  representing the order gives the same coefficient-and-basis sum, independently
  of enumeration; only `Finite G` is required by the constructor. The mean is
  natural for every original `R[G]`-linear map and is the unique retraction onto
  the fixed vectors among original `R[G]`-endomorphisms. The coefficient-linear
  representation construction is provided by
  `GroupTheory.RepresentationAveragingProjector` in `group-theory`. It agrees
  with Mathlib's `averageMap` when the coefficients form a commutative ring,
  addition is a group, and `Fintype G` and an `Invertible` order are supplied;
  this comparison is in `GroupTheory.RepresentationAveragingProjectorComparison`.
  See [AveragingProjector.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AveragingProjector.lean).

  The dependency's `Representation.averagingProjector_toLinearMap_eq_averageMap`
  identifies the underlying coefficient-linear maps: the normalized norm is
  bundled as a self-intertwiner, whereas `averageMap` is a linear endomorphism.

- **Commuting quotient scalar actions.** On a module annihilated by an ideal,
  the [quotient scalar action](../GroupRings/RingTheory/Module/QuotientActions.lean)
  commutes with every action commuting with the original ring action. Two
  commuting ring actions therefore remain commuting after both scalar rings
  are quotiented by annihilating ideals. The rings may be noncommutative and
  the three carriers have independent universes; no finiteness is required.
  For two-sided ideals these are the quotient-ring module actions. For any
  two-sided ideal `I` of a ring `A`, the ordinary quotient
  `I ⧸ Submodule.comap I.subtype (I ^ 2)` has commuting left and right actions of `A`
  and of `A ⧸ I`, retaining compatible commutative base scalars. Left or right
  Noetherianity of `A` gives Noetherianity of the corresponding action on this
  quotient. See [SquareQuotient.lean](../GroupRings/RingTheory/Ideal/SquareQuotient.lean).

- **Noetherian modules over simple algebras.** A simple algebra over a commutative
  ring that acts on a nontrivial compatible module Noetherian over that ring
  is itself Noetherian, and hence finite, over the base ring. Consequently, a
  simple algebra that is not finite over the base ring has no nontrivial
  compatible module Noetherian over the base ring. See
  [Module.lean](../GroupRings/RingTheory/SimpleRing/Module.lean).

- **Finiteness from a nilpotent ideal action.** Let `S` and `A` be rings,
  `I` a two-sided ideal of `A`, and `V` a left `A`-module Noetherian over `A`.
  Give `A` and `V` compatible left `S`-module structures, with scalar towers
  `S → A → A` and `S → A → V`. If `A ⧸ I` is finite over `S` and
  `∃ n, I^n ≤ Module.annihilator A V`, then `V` is finite over `S`.
  Neither a surjective scalar homomorphism nor nilpotence of `I` itself is
  required; only the uniform ideal **action** must be nilpotent. No
  Noetherianity of `S`, commutativity or nontriviality is assumed. See
  `Ideal.finite_of_pow_le_annihilator` in
  [ScalarRestriction.lean](../GroupRings/RingTheory/Ideal/ScalarRestriction.lean).
  The semiprimary specialization recovers Mathlib's finiteness criterion.

- **Canonical representations of given modules.** Over any possibly
  noncommutative or zero semiring `R` and any monoid `M`, a given unital
  `R[M]`-module has a representation on its original carrier with exactly the
  canonical coefficient restriction. Original monoid-algebra linear maps are
  additively equivalent to coefficient-linear intertwiners between these
  representations, without changing their values; the modules may be arbitrary
  additive commutative monoids in independent universes. Packaging preserves
  identity and composition, and the equivalence reflects existence of lifts
  `q.comp f = h` for original module maps, without assuming surjectivity or
  projectivity. See
  [ModuleRepresentationEquiv.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/ModuleRepresentationEquiv.lean).
  For commutative coefficients the inverse agrees with Mathlib's Hom
  correspondence through both canonical `asModuleEquiv` transports; the
  [representation construction](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/ModuleRepresentation.lean)
  also agrees pointwise with Mathlib's scalar-restriction constructions.
  For a semisimple module of a finite `p`-group algebra over any ring with
  prime `p = 0`, this canonical representation is trivial.

- **Projectivity from coefficient projectivity.** For a finite group `G` whose
  order is a unit in any possibly noncommutative or zero semiring `R`, a given
  `R[G]`-module that is projective under its canonical coefficient restriction
  is projective over `R[G]`. Additive commutative monoids and independent
  coefficient, group and module universes suffice. See
  [Projective.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/Projective.lean).

- **Semisimplicity under a nilpotent ideal action.** For arbitrary rings `S`,
  `A`, a unital map `φ : S →+* A` surjective modulo a two-sided ideal `I`,
  and any given `A`-module `V` with `∃ n, I^n ≤ Module.annihilator A V`,
  semisimplicity over `A` is equivalent to `I` annihilating `V` together with
  semisimplicity under the exact restriction `Module.compHom V φ`. This is
  nilpotence of the action, not necessarily of the ideal; the exponent may be
  zero. The ideal action also lies in the module's Jacobson radical, and the
  same characterization holds under that weaker radical condition. See
  [Semisimple.lean](../GroupRings/RingTheory/Ideal/Semisimple.lean).
  For any ring `R`, prime `p` with `(p : R) = 0`, finite `p`-group `G` and
  given `R[G]`-module, semisimplicity is equivalent to canonical coefficient
  semisimplicity **and** trivial canonical group action. In particular, every
  group symbol fixes every vector of a semisimple module. See
  [SemisimpleModules.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/SemisimpleModules.lean).
  Neither result assumes commutativity, nontriviality, finite generation or
  finite length, and the scalar rings and module have independent universes.

- **Scalar restriction and module length.** For any rings `S`, `A`, homomorphism
  `φ : S →+* A`, and given left `A`-module `V`, let `I` be a two-sided ideal
  with `∃ n, I^n ≤ Module.annihilator A V` and surjective canonical map
  `S → A/I`. Noetherianity and Artinianity are each equivalent over `A` and
  the exact restriction `Module.compHom V φ`; their composition lengths agree
  in `ℕ∞`, including infinity. In particular this holds for nilpotent `I`.
  The map `φ` need not be injective, split, surjective itself or have central
  image. See [ScalarRestriction.lean](../GroupRings/RingTheory/Ideal/ScalarRestriction.lean).
  For any ring `R`, prime `p` with `(p : R) = 0`, finite `p`-group `G`, and
  arbitrary given `R[G]`-module, all three comparisons hold with its canonical
  coefficient restriction to `R`. There is no field, commutativity, finite
  generation or finite-length assumption. See
  [ModuleFiniteness.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/ModuleFiniteness.lean).
  Only annihilated layers have equal submodule lattices, not generally the
  whole module. The length is relative to the scalar ring, not an underlying
  field dimension.

- **Detection of module maps.** Let `R` be any possibly noncommutative or zero
  ring, `p` a prime with `(p : R) = 0`, and `G` a finite `p`-group. Every
  `R[G]`-linear map is injective exactly when its canonical fixed-vector map is
  injective, and surjective exactly when its canonical coinvariant map is
  surjective. The modules may lie in independent universes and need not be
  finitely generated. Fixed vectors are the augmentation ideal's common
  annihilator with the canonical coefficient restriction; coinvariants are
  the original quotient by `I • ⊤`. See
  [ModuleDetection.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/ModuleDetection.lean).
  The [general ideal-module results](../GroupRings/RingTheory/Ideal/ModuleDetection.lean)
  give injectivity detection for nilpotent two-sided ideals over semirings
  acting on additive groups, and surjectivity detection for nilpotent left
  ideals over rings. Nilpotent Nakayama itself allows arbitrary semimodules.
  Neither individual functor is asserted to reflect all isomorphisms.

## Subgroup ideals and coset bases

- **Subgroup augmentation ideals.** For any possibly noncommutative ring `R`,
  group `G` and subgroup `H`, the [left ideal of subgroup differences](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/SubgroupIdeals.lean)
  consists exactly of elements with zero coefficient sums on every left coset
  `gH`. The coset-sum map itself needs only a semiring. Over a **nontrivial**
  ring, these ideals reflect subgroup membership and inclusion, and are
  two-sided exactly when `H` is normal. Characteristic membership, forward
  inclusion and normality implying two-sidedness also allow the zero ring.
  No commutativity or finiteness is assumed; the [guide](SubgroupIdeals.md)
  explains the coefficient module, hypotheses and nonnormal matrix-ring example.

- **Finite generation of subgroup ideals.** Over any ring, a finitely generated
  subgroup gives a [finitely generated left group-ring ideal](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/SubgroupIdealsFG.lean).
  Over a **nontrivial** ring the converse holds, without normality or
  commutativity. In particular, the augmentation kernel is finitely generated
  as a left ideal exactly when the group is finitely generated. The forward
  implication allows the zero ring. This is finite generation over `R[G]`,
  not over `R`; the [public-import client](../tests/GroupRingsTest/SubgroupIdealsFGClient.lean)
  illustrates matrix coefficients, an infinite cyclic group and the zero-ring
  boundary for an infinite-rank free abelian group.

- **Subgroup-coset bases.** For any semiring `R`, group `G` and subgroup `H`,
  `R[G]` has a basis as a left `R[H]`-module indexed by right cosets `H * g`.
  Its vectors are single terms at chosen representatives; the coordinate at
  `q` has coefficient `f.coeff (h * q.out)` at `h : H`. The
  [subgroup-basis module](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/SubgroupBasis.lean) provides
  finitely supported coordinates, reconstruction and left scalar compatibility.
  Finite index gives `Module.Finite` over arbitrary semirings, including zero
  and noncommutative ones. For **ring** coefficients, finite index gives
  `IsNoetherianRing (R[G]) ↔ IsNoetherianRing (R[H])`. No normality, finite-group
  or numerical-rank hypothesis is imposed.

## Coefficient and index quotients

- **Powers of coefficient kernels.** For any homomorphism `f : R →+* S`
  between possibly zero or noncommutative semirings and any monoid `M`, the
  kernel of `mapRingHom M f` has zero `n`-th power exactly when `ker f` does,
  at the same exponent, including `n = 0`. No surjectivity, finite monoid or
  group hypothesis is needed. See
  [KernelPowers.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/KernelPowers.lean).

- **Normal-subgroup quotients.** For any ring `R`, group `G` and normal subgroup
  `N`, the canonical index map identifies `R[G/N]` with the quotient of `R[G]`
  by the left span of `single n 1 - 1`. The equivalence preserves every
  coefficient. A unital ring homomorphism into any semiring factors uniquely
  exactly when it sends all `single n 1` to one. No commutativity,
  nontriviality or finiteness is assumed; see the [quotient guide](Quotient.md).

- **Kernel and range quotients.** For any group-to-monoid homomorphism `φ`,
  the unchanged-coefficient map on group rings has kernel the left span of
  `single n 1 - 1` for `n ∈ φ.ker`. The quotient is canonically ring-equivalent
  to the algebra of the range submonoid, with full coefficient formulas and
  unique factorization into any semiring. Surjective `φ` also gives the whole
  target algebra. The coefficient ring may be noncommutative or zero, and no
  group structure on the target is assumed; see the
  [kernel and range guide](KernelRange.md).

- **Coefficient change.** For any unital homomorphism `φ : R →+* S` from
  a possibly noncommutative ring to a semiring, and any monoid `M`,
  [coefficient change](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/CoefficientChange.lean)
  maps the augmentation ideal of `R[M]` onto that of `S[M]` as an ideal.
  The homomorphism need not be surjective. Naturality and the kernel
  preimage formula hold for arbitrary semiring coefficients.

- **Coefficient ideals and kernels.** For any semiring `R`, monoid `M`, and
  ordinary **left** ideal `I`, extension along the constants map consists
  exactly of elements whose every coefficient belongs to `I`. Thus for any
  homomorphism `f : R →+* S` between independently universed semirings,
  `ker (mapRingHom M f) = Ideal.map singleOneRingHom (ker f)`, with no
  commutativity, inverses, finiteness, nontriviality or surjectivity assumption.
  With **Ring R**, **Semiring S** and surjective `f`, the quotient is `S[M]`;
  without surjectivity it is the map's range subsemiring. The
  [coefficient-ideal module](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/CoefficientIdeals.lean)
  supplies membership, all-basis, class and uniqueness formulas. The
  [polynomial transport](../GroupRings/Polynomial/CoefficientIdeals.lean) preserves
  all coefficients, constants, ideals and coefficient change.

- **Simultaneous coefficient/index kernels.** For **Ring R**, **Semiring S**,
  **Group G**, **Monoid M**, `f : R →+* S` and `φ : G →* M`, the
  [mixed-map kernel](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/MixedQuotient.lean) is the sum of
  the left span of `single n 1 - 1` for `n ∈ φ.ker` and the extended coefficient
  kernel. Neither map need be surjective, and `M` need not be cancellative or
  a group. Under surjective `f`, the quotient is `S[MonoidHom.mrange φ]`;
  if `φ` is also surjective it is `S[M]`. For arbitrary maps the target is the
  composite's range subsemiring. No nontriviality or commutativity is assumed;
  all four universes are independent. The [guide](CoefficientIdeals.md)
  explains the factorization and collision boundary.

## Central summands finite over the base ring

Let `R` be a commutative ring and `A` a possibly noncommutative `R`-algebra
that is Artinian on both the left and the right. Every `R`-algebra character
`χ : A →ₐ[R] R` has a central idempotent `e` with `χ e = 1` whose principal
left ideal `Ideal.span ({e} : Set A)` is finitely generated over `R`, with
the inherited scalar action. The base ring need not be a field or reduced,
and the whole algebra need not be finite over it. The zero ring is allowed,
the idempotent need not be primitive, and `R` and `A` have independent
universes. See
[FiniteCharacterBlock.lean](../GroupRings/RingTheory/Artinian/FiniteCharacterBlock.lean)
and the [proof strategy](Usage.md#central-summands-detected-by-characters).

## Uniform binomial kernels

For arbitrary monoids `N`, `M`, any homomorphism `f : N →* M`, and any ring `R`,
`MonoidAlgebra.ker_mapDomainRingHom_eq_span_fiber` identifies the induced
monoid-algebra kernel with the left ideal spanned by all
`single a 1 - single b 1` satisfying `f a = f b`.

When `N` is commutative and finitely generated,
`MonoidAlgebra.uniform_fiber_span_of_fg` chooses one finite set of such index
pairs **before** quantifying over the coefficient ring. The same pairs generate
all these kernels, including over noncommutative, zero or non-Noetherian rings.
The target monoid need not commute, be finitely generated, or be covered by `f`.
The source, target and coefficient universes are independent.

The more general `MonoidAlgebra.uniform_fiber_span_of_integer_kernel_fg` needs
only monoid structures on source and target, with finite generation of the
integer kernel as an explicit premise. Finite extraction is made over the
integers, then unit-coefficient binomials and ideal spans are transported;
arbitrary coefficient-ring relations are not assumed to be integer images.
See [the all-fiber theorem](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/BinomialKernel.lean),
[the finite-generation theorems](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/UniformBinomialKernel.lean)
and [coefficientwise normalization](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/FiberNormalization.lean).

## Relative module finiteness

For any semiring `R` and monoids `M`, `N`, all in independent universes, and
homomorphism `f : M →* N`,
[`MonoidAlgebra.moduleFinite_mapDomainRingHom_iff`](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/MapDomainFinite.lean)
states:

```lean
@Module.Finite (MonoidAlgebra R M) (MonoidAlgebra R N)
  inferInstance inferInstance ((MonoidAlgebra.mapDomainRingHom R f).toModule) ↔
  (∃ T : Set N, T.Finite ∧ ∀ n : N, ∃ m : M, ∃ t ∈ T, n = f m * t) ∨
    Subsingleton R
```

This is the target's **left regular action restricted along the given map**:
`a • p = mapDomainRingHom R f a * p`. It is not an assertion about an arbitrary
module action, `RingHom.Finite`, or a new algebra structure. The cover has the
source image on the **left**, `Set.range f * T`, not `T * Set.range f`.
Neither coefficients nor monoids need commute. No ring, cancellation, group,
Noetherianity, finite generation or finite-carrier hypothesis is imposed.

With `[Nontrivial R]`, `moduleFinite_mapDomainRingHom_iff_of_nontrivial` removes
the subsingleton alternative. A finite cover gives the coefficient-one module
generators `single t 1`, `t ∈ T`, even for zero coefficients; this is
`moduleFinite_mapDomainRingHom_of_finite_cover`. Conversely,
`exists_finite_cover_of_moduleFinite_mapDomainRingHom` extracts a finite
remainder set from the union of coefficient supports of module generators,
under nontrivial coefficients.

The pointwise engine
[`mem_span_single_mapDomainRingHom_iff`](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/MapDomainSpan.lean)
works for **any** label set `T : Set N`: for the same restricted action, `p`
lies in the `R[M]`-span of `{single t 1 | t ∈ T}` exactly when
`(↑p.coeff.support : Set N) ⊆ Set.range f * T`.
The set need not be finite, multiplicatively closed or stable under scalars.
For empty labels, membership in the zero span is equivalent to empty support.
This support criterion includes zero coefficients without an extra alternative;
finite generation of the whole target requires a finite covering remainder set.

Identity and surjective maps have remainder set `{1}`, even for monoids that
are not finitely generated. Surjectivity is not necessary: doubling on the
additive monoid `ℕ`, viewed multiplicatively, has remainders `0` and `1`.
For a map from the one-element monoid to an infinite monoid there is no finite
cover, but subsingleton coefficients still give a finite, trivial target
module. With nontrivial commutative coefficients and target `Multiplicative ℕ`,
the coefficient algebra is of finite type but is not module-finite along that
one-element-source map. Thus this criterion is distinct from algebra finite
type and from finite generation of the target monoid.

For a commutative semiring `R` and submonoids `S ≤ U` of any monoid `M`, the
[monomial-subalgebra API](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/MonomialSubalgebra.lean)
retains its support-membership characterization. For any set `E : Set M`, the
span of `single e 1`, `e ∈ E`, over `monomialSubalgebra R S`, restricted to `R`,
equals `supported R R ((S : Set M) * E)`.
The [finite-cover criterion](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/MonomialFinite.lean)
uses the original inclusion-induced left action: over nontrivial coefficients,
`monomialSubalgebra R U` is finite over `monomialSubalgebra R S` exactly when
there is a finite `E ⊆ U` with `U ⊆ S * E`. Sufficiency also allows zero
coefficients. These are specializations of the map-domain engine, not a
separate span or cover criterion; the subalgebra objects retain their
commutative-coefficient hypothesis, without exponent commutativity or
cancellation. See the [usage inventory](Usage.md#relative-module-finiteness)
and [credits](../CREDITS.md#relative-module-finiteness-and-monomial-subalgebras).

## Finite type

For any commutative semiring `R` and monoid `M` in independent universes,
`MonoidAlgebra.finiteType_iff_fg_or_subsingleton` states

```lean
Algebra.FiniteType R (MonoidAlgebra R M) ↔ Monoid.FG M ∨ Subsingleton R
```

The algebra is the canonical coefficient algebra, with its possibly
noncommutative semiring target. No commutativity, cancellation, group or
finite-carrier hypothesis is imposed on `M`, and `R` need not be a ring or
Noetherian. The zero-coefficient alternative is essential. With `[Nontrivial R]`,
`MonoidAlgebra.finiteType_iff_fg_of_nontrivial` gives finite type iff
`Monoid.FG M`, recovering Mathlib's ring criterion at `CommRing` coefficients.
Finite generation gives a forward finite-type instance; there is no converse
finite-generation instance. The additive counterparts are transported through
Mathlib's coefficient-preserving multiplicative type-tag algebra equivalence.
This concerns algebra finite type, not module finiteness or finite presentation.
See the [module](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/FiniteType.lean) and
[public-import client](../tests/GroupRingsTest/PublicAPIClient.lean), which uses
`ℕ` coefficients and the noncommutative two-letter free monoid.

## Finite presentation

For any commutative ring `R` and finitely generated commutative monoid `M`,
`MonoidAlgebra.finitePresentation_of_fg` supplies
`Algebra.FinitePresentation R (MonoidAlgebra R M)` for the canonical scalar algebra.
`AddMonoidAlgebra.finitePresentation_of_fg` supplies the additive counterpart.
Coefficient and exponent universes are independent; no Noetherianity,
cancellation, nontriviality or finite-carrier assumption is needed.
See the [module](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/FinitePresentation.lean) and
[public-import clients](../tests/GroupRingsTest/PublicAPIClient.lean), which use
scalar base change and additive `ℕ` over the non-Noetherian ring `ℕ → ℤ`.

## Chain conditions and regularity

- **Artinian descent under coefficient retractions.** For semirings `R`, `S`,
  any monoid `M`, and a unital `φ : R →+* S`, suppose an additive
  `E : S →+ R` satisfies `E (φ r) = r` and
  `E (s * φ r) = E s * r`. Left Artinianity of `S[M]` then implies left
  Artinianity of `R[M]`. No multiplicative retraction, left-linearity,
  coefficient commutativity or finite monoid is required. This is regular-left
  Artinianity, not Artinianity after coefficient restriction. See
  `MonoidAlgebra.isArtinianRing_of_coefficient_additive_retraction` in
  [CoefficientChange.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/CoefficientChange.lean).
  For any homomorphism `D →+* S` from a division ring to a nontrivial semiring,
  a normalized right-linear functional supplies this retraction. Thus
  `MonoidAlgebra.isArtinianRing_of_divisionRing_hom` gives the same descent for
  every monoid, without coefficient centrality, finite dimension over the centre,
  or Artinianity after restriction of scalars.

- **Left ideals finite over the coefficients.** If `R` is any semiring and `M`
  is a cancellative monoid, a nonzero left ideal of `MonoidAlgebra R M` that
  is finite as an `R`-module forces `M` to be finite. The module hypothesis
  uses the ordinary coefficient restriction `I.restrictScalars R`; coefficients
  need not commute or form a nontrivial semiring. A supporting lemma bounds
  the supports of every finitely generated coefficient submodule by one finite
  set. See [FiniteCoefficientIdeal.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/FiniteCoefficientIdeal.lean).

- **Finitely generated commutative monoid algebras.** For any ring `R` and
  finitely generated commutative monoid `M`,
  `MonoidAlgebra.isNoetherianRing_iff_of_fg` gives
  `IsNoetherianRing (R[M]) ↔ IsNoetherianRing R`.
  The coefficient ring may be noncommutative or zero, the two types have
  independent universes, and `M` need not be finite. This combines the
  existing `MonoidAlgebra.isNoetherianRing_of_fg` ascent with surjective
  augmentation; the ascent uses a finite free-commutative-monoid presentation
  and finite-coordinate Noetherianity from `multivariate-polynomials`.

- **Commutative groups and finite-index extensions.** For any ring `R` and
  commutative group `G`, `MonoidAlgebra.isNoetherianRing_iff_of_commGroup` gives
  `IsNoetherianRing (R[G]) ↔
  IsNoetherianRing R ∧ (Subsingleton R ∨ Group.FG G)`.
  The zero-ring alternative is essential; no decomposition of `G` is supplied
  or required. For a subgroup `H` of any group `G`, with `[H.FiniteIndex]`,
  `[IsMulCommutative H]` and `[Group.FG H]`,
  `MonoidAlgebra.isNoetherianRing_iff_of_finiteIndex_comm_fg H` gives
  `IsNoetherianRing (R[G]) ↔ IsNoetherianRing R`. These hypotheses concern
  the inherited subgroup operations, without normality or ambient
  commutativity. Both criteria allow noncommutative coefficients and
  independent universes and concern the regular **left** module, not the
  coefficient module. See the [Noetherian guide](Noetherian.md).

- **Left-Artinian descent along additive right retractions.** For semirings
  `A`, `B` in independent universes, a unital homomorphism `f : A →+* B`
  and additive map `E : B →+ A` with `E 1 = 1` and
  `E (b * f a) = E b * a` give extension-contraction equality and order
  reflection for ordinary left ideals. Thus left Artinianity of `B` implies
  left Artinianity of `A`. No commutativity, nontriviality or additive inverses
  are required, and `E` need not preserve multiplication or be left-linear.
  The API uses the equivalent retraction condition `E (f a) = a`.
  See [Artinian.lean](../GroupRings/RingTheory/Artinian.lean).

- **Centrality of idempotents through nilpotent ideals.** Let `A` be any
  possibly zero or noncommutative ring, `I` a nilpotent two-sided ideal, and
  `e` an idempotent whose image is central in `A ⧸ I`. If both corner maps
  `x ↦ e * x * (1 - e)` and `x ↦ (1 - e) * x * e` send `I` into `I²`, then
  `e` is central in `A`. Each first-layer corner condition separately raises
  every positive ideal-power layer, without requiring nilpotence or quotient
  centrality for that propagation. Both conditions are retained in
  `IsIdempotentElem.isMulCentral_of_sandwich_mem_sq`; quotient centrality
  alone is not a central-lifting theorem. See
  [IdempotentCentrality.lean](../GroupRings/RingTheory/IdempotentCentrality.lean).

- **Left-Noetherian group subalgebras.** For any semiring `R`, group `H`, monoid
  `M` and injective `j : H →* M` in independent universes, left-Noetherianity of
  `R[M]` implies that of `R[H]`. Thus every subgroup algebra of a left-Noetherian
  group algebra is left-Noetherian, without finite index or normality. No
  commutativity, nontriviality, additive inverses, cancellation or target-group
  assumption is needed. The [Noetherian guide](Noetherian.md) describes the
  additive right retraction, ideal extension-contraction and the distinction
  between right compatibility and ordinary regular-left Noetherianity.

- **Left-Noetherian necessary conditions.** If `R[M]` is left-Noetherian, then
  `R` is left-Noetherian, for any semiring `R` and monoid `M`: augmentation is
  surjective, so Mathlib's generic surjective-homomorphism theorem applies.
  Over a **nontrivial**, possibly noncommutative ring `R`, left-Noetherianity
  of `R[G]` implies that **every** subgroup of `G` is finitely generated.
  This uses the algebra as a left module over itself, not over `R`.
  See the [Noetherian module](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/Noetherian.lean)
  and [public-import client](../tests/GroupRingsTest/NoetherianClient.lean).
  No converse for arbitrary groups is asserted.

- **Cyclic and finite-index Noetherianity.** For every ring `R`, including
  noncommutative and zero rings, `R[Multiplicative ℤ]` is left-Noetherian iff
  `R` is. An equivalence with the infinite cyclic group, or a finite-index
  infinite cyclic subgroup, gives the same criterion, without normality.
  This includes products with finite groups. Import
  `GroupRings.MonoidAlgebra.CyclicNoetherian` directly; the root does not
  re-export it. These specialize the commutative-monoid and finite-index
  criteria above; the public Laurent import is preserved. See
  [credits](../CREDITS.md) for the earlier Laurent proof's lineage.

- **Regular monoid-algebra differences and an Artinian obstruction.** For an
  arbitrary ring `R` and a cancellative monoid `M`, if `g : M` has infinite
  order, then `single g 1 - single 1 r` is left- and right-regular for every
  coefficient `r : R`. The additive-index version specializes to Laurent
  polynomials, without a domain or commutativity assumption. Consequently, if
  `R` is nontrivial and `MonoidAlgebra R M` is left Artinian over itself, every
  element of `M` has finite order. This is a necessary condition, not a claim
  that `M` is finite. See
  [Regular.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/Regular.lean).

- **Left-Artinian right-cancellative monoid algebras.** For any ring `R` and
  monoid `G` with `[IsRightCancelMul G]`,
  `MonoidAlgebra.isArtinianRing_iff` gives
  `IsArtinianRing (MonoidAlgebra R G) ↔
  IsArtinianRing R ∧ (Subsingleton R ∨ Finite G)`.
  `MonoidAlgebra.finite_of_isArtinianRing` gives monoid finiteness directly
  when `R` is nontrivial. These are conditions on the original left regular
  modules; coefficient commutativity, right Artinianity, finite generation
  and a finite-degree condition over the centre are not hypotheses. The
  universes are independent and the zero-ring alternative is essential.
  Groups specialize to the classical Artinian group-ring criterion; see
  Passman, *The Algebraic Structure of Group Rings*.

  The local theorem
  `MonoidAlgebra.isUnit_of_isRightRegular_of_isArtinianRing` works over any
  **nontrivial semiring** and any monoid: left Artinianity of `R[M]` makes a
  specified right-regular `m : M` a unit, without global cancellation.
  Injective right reindexing and Mathlib's Artinian regular-element criterion
  give a unit basis term; the single-term unit criterion reflects its index.
  For a right-cancellative monoid this makes the canonical units coercion
  bijective, reducing to the group criterion by a ring equivalence. The
  original monoid operations are retained. The global iff still requires a
  **ring**: Artinian ascent along finite modules fails for general semirings.
  See [Artinian.lean](../GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/Artinian.lean) and the
  [usage guide](Usage.md#artinian-monoid-algebras) for the units reduction
  and the simple-quotient, centre-field and finite-support group proof.

- **Artinian Laurent polynomial rings.** For every possibly noncommutative or zero
  ring `R`, the original Laurent polynomial ring `LaurentPolynomial R` is left
  Artinian over itself exactly when `R` is subsingleton. The supporting API proves
  that `T 1 - C r` is regular for every coefficient `r`, and that `T 1 - 1` is
  not a unit over a nontrivial ring. No domain or commutativity assumption is
  needed. See [Artinian.lean](../GroupRings/LaurentPolynomial/Artinian.lean).

## Laurent coordinates

- **Laurent coordinates.** For a commutative semiring `A`, additive commutative
  group `G`, and *supplied* equivalence `G ≃+ (σ →₀ ℤ)`, the
  [coordinate equivalence](../GroupRings/FreeAbelian/LaurentPolynomial.lean#L35)
  identifies `MonoidAlgebra A (Multiplicative G)` with
  `MvLaurentPolynomial σ A = AddMonoidAlgebra A (σ →₀ ℤ)`. The
  [forward](../GroupRings/FreeAbelian/LaurentPolynomial.lean#L44) and
  [inverse](../GroupRings/FreeAbelian/LaurentPolynomial.lean#L60) basis formulas
  give the image of every single term. The Laurent abbreviation itself needs
  only `[Semiring A]`; no choice of coordinates is proved. Specializing along
  `FreeAbelianGroup.equivFinsupp` gives the
  [canonical equivalence](../GroupRings/FreeAbelian/LaurentPolynomial.lean#L76),
  its [forward](../GroupRings/FreeAbelian/LaurentPolynomial.lean#L84) and
  [inverse](../GroupRings/FreeAbelian/LaurentPolynomial.lean#L95) single-term laws,
  and a [generator simp law](../GroupRings/FreeAbelian/LaurentPolynomial.lean#L106)
  at priority 1100. Each exponent has finite support; `σ` itself can be empty
  or infinite, and negative exponents and the zero commutative semiring are
  permitted. This does not establish projective-module freeness or K-theory.

## Characters and right convolution

- **Field-valued character independence and bases.** Characters of an additive
  commutative `G` have [linearly independent evaluations](../GroupRings/FiniteAbelian/RightConvolution.lean#L43)
  over any field, even when `G` is infinite. A finite `G` has finitely many
  characters over any field; enough roots of unity of exponent
  `Monoid.exponent (Multiplicative G)` then yield the
  [cardinality theorem](../GroupRings/FiniteAbelian/RightConvolution.lean#L66)
  and [evaluation basis](../GroupRings/FiniteAbelian/RightConvolution.lean#L74).

- **Right convolution, eigenvalues and rank.** With `[Fintype G]`, the
  [right-convolution matrix](../GroupRings/FiniteAbelian/RightConvolution.lean#L105)
  has entries `f (-sigma + tau)` and positive-character eigenvalues
  `∑ x, f x * psi x` without a roots assumption for its
  [eigenvector law](../GroupRings/FiniteAbelian/RightConvolution.lean#L113).
  Under enough roots it admits [diagonalization](../GroupRings/FiniteAbelian/RightConvolution.lean#L137)
  and a [Nat.card rank formula](../GroupRings/FiniteAbelian/RightConvolution.lean#L161).
  The [Fourier guide](FiniteAbelianRightConvolution.md) gives exact
  hypotheses, the positive sign and client examples.

## Mathematical scope

`MvLaurentPolynomial σ A` is an abbreviation for
`AddMonoidAlgebra A (σ →₀ ℤ)`: a monomial has a finitely supported vector of
integer exponents. The variable type itself may be empty, finite or infinite;
negative exponents are allowed. This model needs only a semiring `A`, without
commutativity or nontriviality.

For a commutative coefficient semiring `A`, an additive commutative group `G`,
and supplied coordinates `e : G ≃+ (σ →₀ ℤ)`, the API constructs

```lean
MonoidAlgebra A (Multiplicative G) ≃ₐ[A] MvLaurentPolynomial σ A
```

It first reverses mathlib's additive-to-multiplicative group-algebra equivalence,
then transports the exponent group along `e`. The canonical specialization takes
`G = FreeAbelianGroup σ` and uses `FreeAbelianGroup.equivFinsupp σ`. The coefficient,
group and index universes are independent in the supplied-coordinate form.

The API requires no decidable equality, finite generation, field, domain, ring or
nonzero-coefficient assumption. Its equivalences also apply to the zero
commutative semiring. It does not choose coordinates from an unbundled existential
freeness assertion.

This library does **not** prove projective-module freeness, Laurent-PID structure,
Quillen–Suslin or Bass–Quillen, stable freeness or K-theory. An algebra equivalence
alone does not prove projectives free.

Separately, for an additive commutative group `G` and field `K` (in independent
universes), evaluations by `AddChar G K` are linearly independent without a
finite-group or enough-roots hypothesis. For finite `G`, character finiteness
holds over any field. If the field has enough roots of unity of exponent
`Monoid.exponent (Multiplicative G)`, character evaluations give a basis of
`G → K`. For `[Fintype G]`, right convolution has matrix entry
`f (-sigma + tau)` and character eigenvalue `∑ x, f x * psi x`; its eigenvector
law needs no enough-roots assumption. Under enough roots, the character basis
diagonalizes convolution and its rank is the `Nat.card` of characters with
nonzero eigenvalue. This does not identify convolution with a group-algebra
operator or assert a nonabelian Fourier basis.

For any semiring `R` and monoid `M`, `MonoidAlgebra.augmentation R M` sends an
algebra element to the sum of its coefficients. If `R` is a ring, its kernel is
`Ideal.span (Set.range (fun m : M => MonoidAlgebra.single m (1 : R) - 1))`.
Here `Ideal.span` is a left ideal: the coefficient expansion uses
`single 1 r * (single m 1 - 1)`, with the coefficient on the left. The
[augmentation client](../tests/GroupRingsTest/AugmentationClient.lean) independently
exhibits a nonzero kernel element over `ℤ` and the natural-number monoid, and
uses the span theorem with arbitrary left coefficients and monoid indices.
The [generating-set client](../tests/GroupRingsTest/AugmentationGeneratorsClient.lean)
uses two generators of `FreeMonoid Bool` with noncommutative matrix coefficients,
obtains finite generation directly from finite image and `Submodule.fg_span`,
and checks that the infinite cyclic group's inverse difference lies in the span
of the positive generator difference despite failure of monoid generation.
The [coefficient-basis client](../tests/GroupRingsTest/AugmentationBasisClient.lean)
uses the vector and coordinate laws over a matrix ring and `Multiplicative ℕ`,
distinguishing left scalar multiplication from right multiplication. The
semiring-level `sumCoords_basis_apply` and `ker_augmentation_restrictScalars`
bridges identify Mathlib's coordinate sum with augmentation.

The [single-term client](../tests/GroupRingsTest/SingleUnitsClient.lean) constructs
an independent nonidentity unit over `ℕ` at the positive generator of
`Multiplicative ℤ`, checking its inverse directly from multiplication laws.
It also uses the single-term criterion to rule out invertibility of the basis
term at `2` in `MonoidAlgebra ℕ ℕ`, whose index monoid is not a group. The zero
coefficient semiring explains the nontriviality hypothesis for index reflection:
its monoid algebra is trivial, so every basis image is a unit regardless of its
index.
